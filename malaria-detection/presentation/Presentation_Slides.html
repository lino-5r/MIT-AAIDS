The Problem Malaria kills 400,000 people per year. The bottleneck is diagnosis.
  • 229 million cases and ~400,000 deaths globally per year (WHO 2019). 94% of deaths occur in Sub-Saharan Africa; 67% are children under five.
  • Standard diagnosis requires a trained microscopist to manually examine Giemsa-stained blood smear slides under a microscope, cell by cell.
  • Each slide takes 5–15 minutes to examine properly. A lab handling 100 slides per day needs 8–25 hours of skilled microscopist time.
  • In high-burden regions, that expertise is scarce, unevenly distributed, and subject to fatigue. Inter-rater agreement degrades at scale.
  • Result: delayed diagnoses, missed infections, ongoing transmission.
The core error

A missed infection (false negative) sends a patient home without treatment. The infection progresses; the patient transmits. A false alarm (false positive) triggers a follow-up test. These errors are not symmetric. In this project, minimizing false negatives is the governing objective.


The AI opportunity

Classify individual cell images as Parasitized or Uninfected automatically, at any scale, with consistent accuracy, on modest hardware. Route only the borderline cases to a human reviewer.

Approach Four models. A deliberate experimental progression. One winner.
1
CNN1 — Baseline
Simple 3-layer CNN. Establishes whether the visual signal is learnable. Trains in 8 epochs. Gives us a baseline to beat.
2
CNN2 — Augmented
Adds data augmentation (rotation, flip, zoom) and architectural improvements (BatchNormalization, GlobalAveragePooling2D). Reduces overfitting. Exposes a calibration problem.
3
CNN3 — Recall-Optimized
Same architecture as CNN2. Adds class weighting (Parasitized at 2× cost) and recall tracking during training. Fixes the calibration failure. Best overall result.
4
MobileNetV2 — Transfer Learning
Pre-trained on 1.28M ImageNet images. Two-phase training (frozen backbone, then fine-tune). The expected winner. Underperformed at 64×64 pixel resolution.
Why this sequence?

Each step changes one variable. CNN1→CNN2 combined augmentation + architecture changes. CNN2→CNN3 changes only the training objective. CNN3 is therefore the first controlled experiment in the sequence, isolating the effect of class weighting.

Evaluation criteria

Recall (% of infected cells correctly identified) is the primary clinical metric — missed infections are the highest-cost error. Overall accuracy must also be high to be clinically trusted. AUC measures discriminative power across all thresholds.

The Key Insight CNN2 and CNN3 have the same architecture. The training objective made the difference.
  • CNN2's problem: The model's confidence scores clustered near zero. At the standard 50% decision threshold, almost every infected cell scored below the cutoff. Result: - missed infections. Yet AUC was 0.98 — the model could distinguish classes. The problem was calibration, not capability.
  • The fix: CNN3 adds a class weight of 2:1 (Parasitized costs twice as much to miss). The optimizer is directly penalized for the failure mode that matters most. The model learns to score infected cells above 0.5.
  • The result: From - missed infections to -, with no change to the architecture, the data pipeline, or the test set.
  • The implication: Architecture complexity is not the limiting factor. Training objective alignment to clinical goals is.

How triage works

Scores below -: auto-classify Uninfected. Above -: auto-classify Parasitized. Between -–-: flag for microscopist review. The window is tunable.

Triage threshold sweep: as the confidence window narrows, fewer cases are referred and recall holds. The selected operating point balances high recall with a minimal human review workload.

Implementation Four phases from pilot to scale
1
Pilot validation (months 1–3)
Deploy CNN3 in parallel with manual review at one partner lab. Run both systems on 2,000+ slides. Confirm false negative rate under real staining conditions.
2
Triage integration (months 3–6)
Integrate CNN3 as first-pass screener in the lab information system. High-confidence cases auto-classified. Borderline - routed to microscopist. Track disagreements.
3
Full slide pipeline (months 6–12)
Add cell segmentation to extract individual cells from whole slides automatically. This closes the remaining manual step and enables fully automated batch processing.
4
Multi-site validation (months 12–18)
Validate across 3–5 partner labs with different equipment and staining protocols. Test on P. vivax-endemic regions. Required before claiming broad deployability.
The model is already small

CNN3 is ~2.1 MB saved to disk. It runs on any modern laptop with no GPU required. Deployment hardware cost per site: $300–$500. No cloud subscription needed for inference.

Estimated time savings

For a lab processing 100 slides/day: manual screening requires 8–25 person-hours/day. CNN3 with triage reduces that to ~8 minutes/day of microscopist time for borderline case review. The other - of cells are handled automatically.

Risks & Challenges Six known limitations, four high-priority next steps

Key risks

Single-source data
One institution, one staining protocol. Cross-site performance not yet validated.
Species gap
Trained on P. falciparum only. Other species have different morphology. Model not validated on P. vivax etc.
No whole-slide pipeline
Requires pre-segmented cell images. Cell extraction from full slides not yet built.
Interpretability
No heatmaps showing why the model flagged a cell. Grad-CAM is a planned addition.
Staining variability
Model may need site-specific fine-tuning if local staining differs from training distribution.
Regulatory pathway
Diagnostic software requires clearance in most markets. Framed as decision support to manage burden.

Priority next steps

  • Cross-site validation on 500–1,000 slides from two additional labs. Required before clinical deployment.
  • Grad-CAM interpretability maps to show which region of the cell drove each prediction. Builds clinician trust.
  • Higher input resolution (128×128 or 224×224). Likely to improve recall and may change the MobileNetV2 comparison.
  • Two-stage detection pipeline: add cell segmentation (Stage 1) before CNN3 classification (Stage 2). Prior art (Hung et al., 2017) shows 98% accuracy vs. 59% for single-stage on the same task.
